You do not know when it becomes like that,
but a few years have gone by and suddenly
it becomes clear to you that your life
is your only life—where are the lofty dreams now?

By name, you are given a designation
because of your job—a teacher, post office clerk,
assistant professor, bank manager, or tax assistant.
The money earned is barely enough to sustain
your daily living. And in that cycle of work,
payday, Sunday, and work— you become one
of the billion members of society. A mule.

There are days you take stock of your condition
and you still feel it— but it is feeble now, the fire
that was once a great flame of all-consuming ambition.
And then comes a quiet urgency to do something
with your life. And it passes away along with
the setting of the sun.

Your life is your only life. And sometimes
you just live with that. No more. No less.
