Jealousy is called 'Thîkthu'  in Hmar;
'thu'  meaning narratives, or what one has to say,
and 'thîk', although I am a native Hmar speaker
I do not know how to translate it simply other than
that feeling when you feel so inadequate,
so unfitted, so out of place, in any situation
 or even with your dearest person for no reason.
So, 'thîkthu'  in Hmar is not simply jealousy.
It also connotes to that feeling of inadequacy,
that feeling of being not enough... emphatically
naked... that unnamed fright that someone
or something is way better than you
and that they could come along anytime
and ruin your paradise.
You are at your vulnerable best. I mean, that's what
'Thîkthu sie'  is in Hmar— to be vulnerable. To be naked.

So, Ṭhamâmpui, if you ever wish to know about
my 'thîkthu'  for you. Please know that it is always
at its highest degree of 'sie'  (wrong, bad, insecure—in Hmar),
and that I do not even know when I claimed you for myself,
just for me alone, only for me... for the rest of my lifetime.
Please forgive my transgression if there's one.

But kindly know that please
when I say 'Ka ngai che'  (I miss you)—
what I really wanted to say is this:
please do not ever belong to anyone but to me,
my world, my jealous world, my jealous heart.
