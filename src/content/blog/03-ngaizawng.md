My poetry doesn't know how to call you
by one final name yet. You have become
the person behind all the endearing names
I learned to call out before.
Maybe because that fateful day I saw you
for the first time, the stars in my eyes
started to belong to the stars in your eyes.
And my heart, you began to own it
long before my poetry came to know that
you were to become its favorite subject to talk about.
What should I call you now, then?
Shall I simply refer to you as 'Ngaizawng'
as it is in your mother tongue Hmar?
'Ngaizawng', if crudely rendered into English,
is a neuter gender word for girlfriend or boyfriend.
But this translation loses so much of you in me
and it doesn't command the essence of the position
you hold in my heart. So, maybe I must dig out
all the roots of your word 'Ngaizawng' to its deepest depth:
'Ngai' means longing, craving, or yearning,
or something you ought to do to live.
It also refers to your dearest person
whose company you value and crave the most.
'Zawng' means eager, fond, or something
you are solicitous about.

My poetry may call you by any other names
but it is in the word 'Ngaizawng' that you are being
called out to your full extent: my person whom I am
always solicitous to yearn for, to live for.
