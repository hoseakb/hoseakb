My poetry doesn't know how to call you<br />
by one final name yet. You have become<br />
the person behind all the endearing names<br />
I learned to call out before.<br />
Maybe because that fateful day I saw you<br />
for the first time, the stars in my eyes<br />
started to belong to the stars in your eyes.<br />
And my heart, you began to own it<br />
long before my poetry came to know that<br />
you were to become its favorite subject to talk about.

What should I call you now, then?<br />
Shall I simply refer to you as 'Ngaizawng'<br />
as it is in your mother tongue Hmar?<br />
'Ngaizawng', if crudely rendered into English,<br />
is a neuter gender word for girlfriend or boyfriend.<br />
But this translation loses so much of you in me<br />
and it doesn't command the essence of the position<br />
you hold in my heart. So, maybe I must dig out<br />
all the roots of your word 'Ngaizawng' to its deepest depth:

'Ngai' means longing, craving, or yearning,<br />
or something you ought to do to live.<br />
It also refers to your dearest person<br />
whose company you value and crave the most.<br />
'Zawng' means eager, fond, or something<br />
you are solicitous about.

My poetry may call you by any other names<br />
but it is in the word 'Ngaizawng' that you are being<br />
called out to your full extent: my person whom I am<br />
always solicitous to yearn for, to live for.
