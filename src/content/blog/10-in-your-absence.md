I do not know what to call this—
 this quiet healing, this soft happiness
that always comes after whenever I meet you
and the rearrangement of the rugged landscape
of my heart on the long way back home
to Muolvaiphei whenever I get the chance
to sit beside you.
Maybe this is how the common human body,
like yours and mine, heals itself.
And the empty chaotic world inside the human heart
yearns for whenever I miss you.

And this side of the evening, under the drizzling sky
in your absence, is still full of you and you and all you.

A wispy after-rain monsoon mist
softly covers the mountain near my house
and the neighborhood frogs, in the nearby
 paddy-field water, are croaking.

My cat came home just a while ago.
And the dishes on my evening meal table
taste like the look in your summer afternoon eyes.
