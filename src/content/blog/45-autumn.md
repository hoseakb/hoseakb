On the other side of the hills
where Parbung, my ancestral village— is,
atleast as how it's used to be in my memory,
I know that the jhum fields now are pretty,
dancing in the yellow autumn wind.

And from where I write this,
there is no autumn. No space and time
for autumn. Neither for pretty jhum yellow fields.
There are, instead, deadlines. Deadlines.
Academic papers to work out. Books to read.
Research to finish. And works, and works to do.

And so when this poem comes to me
I am at first surprised.
But now that I recognized it and its intention,
it's autumn knocking at my door—because
I haven't gotten the chance to give its due
time and space. And so I am glad, I am glad,
I am glad that, like this poem,
you are happily the only autumn I have,
the only reason why my autumn shall
be as beautiful as it is this time.
Jhum fields or not, you are here now.
And that is all that I have ever prayed
and wished for.
