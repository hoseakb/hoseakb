Jealousy is called 'Thîkthu'  in Hmar;<br />
'thu'  meaning narratives, or what one has to say,<br />
and 'thîk', although I am a native Hmar speaker<br />
I do not know how to translate it simply other than<br />
that feeling when you feel so inadequate,<br />
so unfitted, so out of place, in any situation<br />
 or even with your dearest person for no reason.

So, 'thîkthu'  in Hmar is not simply jealousy.<br />
It also connotes to that feeling of inadequacy,<br />
that feeling of being not enough... emphatically<br />
naked... that unnamed fright that someone<br />
or something is way better than you<br />
and that they could come along anytime<br />
and ruin your paradise.<br />
You are at your vulnerable best. I mean, that's what<br />
'Thîkthu sie'  is in Hmar— to be vulnerable. To be naked.

So, Ṭhamâmpui, if you ever wish to know about<br />
my 'thîkthu'  for you. Please know that it is always<br />
at its highest degree of 'sie'  (wrong, bad, insecure—in Hmar),<br />
and that I do not even know when I claimed you for myself,<br />
just for me alone, only for me... for the rest of my lifetime.<br />
Please forgive my transgression if there's one.

But kindly know that please<br />
when I say 'Ka ngai che'  (I miss you)—<br />
what I really wanted to say is this:<br />
please do not ever belong to anyone but to me,<br />
my world, my jealous world, my jealous heart.
