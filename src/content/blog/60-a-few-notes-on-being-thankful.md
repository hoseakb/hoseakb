The English equivalent of "Thank you"<br />
in Hmar is Ka lawm<br />
in Duhlian (or Mizo), Ka lawm<br />
in Thadou, Ka kipah<br />
in Paite, Ka kipak<br />
in Vaiphei, Ka kipak<br />
in Tedim, Ka lungdam<br />
in Gangte, Ka kipak<br />
in Simte, Ka kipaah<br />
in Zou, Ka kipah<br />
Notice the word roots: pah, pak, pa—<br />
it all means "to bloom", "flowering".<br />
In Hmar, blooming is Pâr,<br />
which in Paite is "Pak".

You see, when we give thanks in gratitude<br />
in this corner of the earth, in this corner<br />
of the world where to live is to know conflicts<br />
and its politic; still, when we give our thanks,<br />
we bring our blooming word, our common word<br />
for "flower" or "flowering". In other words,<br />
we are also happy as in "Ka lawm",<br />
(Ka is "I", Lawm is "happy")<br />
So, when we say "Thank you"<br />
in all our Zo mother tongues, we mean to say:<br />
"I am happy to bring you the bloom of my gratitude."
