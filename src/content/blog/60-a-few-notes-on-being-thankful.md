The English equivalent of "Thank you"
in Hmar is Ka lawm
in Duhlian (or Mizo), Ka lawm
in Thadou, Ka kipah
in Paite, Ka kipak
in Vaiphei, Ka kipak
in Tedim, Ka lungdam
in Gangte, Ka kipak
in Simte, Ka kipaah
in Zou, Ka kipah
Notice the word roots: pah, pak, pa—
it all means "to bloom", "flowering".
In Hmar, blooming is Pâr,
which in Paite is "Pak".

You see, when we give thanks in gratitude
in this corner of the earth, in this corner
of the world where to live is to know conflicts
and its politic; still, when we give our thanks,
we bring our blooming word, our common word
for "flower" or "flowering". In other words,
we are also happy as in "Ka lawm",
(Ka is "I", Lawm is "happy")
So, when we say "Thank you"
in all our Zo mother tongues, we mean to say:
"I am happy to bring you the bloom of my gratitude."
