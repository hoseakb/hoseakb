On the other side of the hills<br />
where Parbung, my ancestral village— is,<br />
atleast as how it's used to be in my memory,<br />
I know that the jhum fields now are pretty,<br />
dancing in the yellow autumn wind.

And from where I write this,<br />
there is no autumn. No space and time<br />
for autumn. Neither for pretty jhum yellow fields.<br />
There are, instead, deadlines. Deadlines.<br />
Academic papers to work out. Books to read.<br />
Research to finish. And works, and works to do.

And so when this poem comes to me<br />
I am at first surprised.<br />
But now that I recognized it and its intention,<br />
it's autumn knocking at my door—because<br />
I haven't gotten the chance to give its due<br />
time and space.

And so I am glad, I am glad,<br />
I am glad that, like this poem,<br />
you are happily the only autumn I have,<br />
the only reason why my autumn shall<br />
be as beautiful as it is this time.<br />
Jhum fields or not, you are here now.<br />
And that is all that I have ever prayed<br />
and wished for.
