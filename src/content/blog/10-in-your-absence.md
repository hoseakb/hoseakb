I do not know what to call this—<br />
 this quiet healing, this soft happiness<br />
that always comes after whenever I meet you<br />
and the rearrangement of the rugged landscape<br />
of my heart on the long way back home<br />
to Muolvaiphei whenever I get the chance<br />
to sit beside you.

Maybe this is how the common human body,<br />
like yours and mine, heals itself.<br />
And the empty chaotic world inside the human heart<br />
yearns for whenever I miss you.

And this side of the evening, under the drizzling sky<br />
in your absence, is still full of you and you and all you.

A wispy after-rain monsoon mist<br />
softly covers the mountain near my house<br />
and the neighborhood frogs, in the nearby<br />
 paddy-field water, are croaking.

My cat came home just a while ago.<br />
And the dishes on my evening meal table<br />
taste like the look in your summer afternoon eyes.
